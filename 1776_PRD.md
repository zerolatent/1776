# 1776 — Product Requirements Document

**A public accountability agent for the AI-accelerated regulatory state.**
**The citizen-facing counterpart to Vulcan's Justinian.**

Version 0.3 · Hackathon build spec · Target jurisdiction: State of Texas

---

## TL;DR

Justinian gives agencies, officials, and regulated industry the power to **produce and reform rules at machine speed**. In Texas, Vulcan has also built **SAM** for the Texas Regulatory Efficiency Office — a public chatbot that helps people **navigate** rules, licenses, and permits. What still does not exist is a tool that lets the **governed** see **how those rules got made and whether their voice mattered.** 1776 is that tool.

1776 is an AI agent that traces the lifecycle of a government rule — what officials **said**, how they **voted**, what rule **resulted**, and **whether the public was actually heard** — and renders it legible, searchable, and fully cited. Every claim it makes carries a receipt back to a primary source. It runs on live Texas data.

**What ships in the hackathon:** a working agent over the Texas Register that, for any recent adopted rule, produces a cited, plain-language accountability brief whose flagship output is the "were the people heard?" signal — parsing the agency's own published Comment/Response record and cross-checking it against the rule the agency actually adopted.

**How it is built:** LangGraph orchestrates the investigation as an explicit state machine; LangChain wraps models, tools, structured extraction, and citation-aware generation. As a P1 layer, 1776 adds civic forecasting cards for proposed rules so users can predict outcomes and build reputation when forecasts resolve against public records.

**Why Vulcan should care:** SAM tells Texans *what the rules are*. 1776 tells them *how the rules got that way — and whether anyone listened when they commented.* It is not a competitor to Justinian or SAM; it is the public-interest complement that closes the accountability loop on the same register, in Vulcan's home state. It is the second half of Vulcan's own thesis: *"as AI gets better, our Republic should get better too."*

## 1. Problem statement

A republic runs on a balance: the state's capacity to act must be matched by the public's capacity to see and check that action. That balance has always rested on human-speed friction — a beat reporter at the hearing, a comment period a person could actually read through. Two forces have broken it.

First, the watchdog collapsed. The U.S. has lost roughly 40% of its newspapers in two decades; hundreds of counties have no local news source and over 1,500 have only one. Where local coverage dies, government secrecy measurably rises, because officials know no one is watching. Trust in government sits near a record low of ~17%.

Second — and this is what makes the problem urgent now rather than merely sad — the state's side of the balance is being handed AGI-level tooling. Vulcan's Justinian runs on ~157 billion records; Texas stood up a Regulatory Efficiency Office (SB 14, $22.8M) whose explicit mandate is to reduce rules, and Vulcan built its public-facing SAM chatbot. When rules are being reviewed, rewritten, and cut at machine speed, the volume and velocity of change exceed any human's ability to track it. The producers of law now operate at a clock speed the public cannot match.

**The cost of not solving it:** rules that reshape people's lives — what their water must be tested for, what a small business must file, what protection exists or was removed — change faster than anyone outside the building can follow. The notice-and-comment process, the one channel for public voice in rulemaking, becomes a formality no one can audit. Accountability that cannot run at the speed of power stops being accountability.

This PRD does not re-argue the problem; the problem definition was stress-tested at length before this spec (including the honest finding that meeting *summarization* is a crowded, solved space — this product is deliberately not that; see Non-Goals).

## 2. Positioning: the complement to Justinian (and SAM)

Vulcan's product line arms one side of the democratic loop:

| Vulcan module | Serves | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Justinian | Regulators / agencies | Produce & reform rules |
| Solon | Corporate counsel | Comply / challenge overreach |
| Minerva | Policy teams | Research, process comments |
| Trajan | Government services | Permitting, licensing |
| **SAM** (Texas) | The public | **Navigate** existing rules/licenses |

Every module serves someone either **inside power** or the public *as a rule-follower*. None serves the public as a **rule-watcher** — someone asking not "what is the rule?" but "how did this rule get made, and did my voice count?" That absence is the entire opportunity.

**1776 is the accountability module.** Same underlying corpus — the Texas Register and the Texas legislative record — pointed outward, to the citizen and the journalist. SAM helps you *comply*; 1776 helps you *hold accountable*.

The pitch in one sentence: **"SAM helps Texans find out what the rules are; 1776 helps them find out how the rules got that way — and whether anyone listened when they commented."** Same state, same register, complementary direction.

> **Naming.** *1776* points to the civic founding idea behind the product: public power is accountable to the governed. It is meant to feel broader than a single state or agency while still carrying a clear democratic accountability signal.

## 3. Goals

Outcomes, not outputs.

1. **Make regulatory change legible.** A non-expert can understand what a given rule changes, who it affects, and what stage it's at — in under two minutes, from primary sources.
2. **Surface whether public comment mattered.** For any adopted rule, 1776 shows the concerns the public raised, the agency's stated response, and whether the adopted text is consistent with that response. This is the flagship accountability signal and the thing no incumbent (including SAM) does.
3. **Guarantee verifiability.** Every factual claim 1776 outputs links to a primary-source artifact (a specific Texas Register issue/section, TAC citation, or timestamped video). Zero uncited claims. This is both the trust mechanism and the defamation firewall — and it matches Vulcan's own bar ("verifies and cites every claim").
4. **Demonstrate the full lineage.** For at least one real example, connect a rule back through its authorizing bill (and, as a stretch, to what officials said on the record) — proving the "say → vote → rule" pipeline is real, not conceptual.
5. **Capture live civic foresight.** For proposed rules, let informed users forecast likely outcomes using play-money/reputation mechanics, then resolve those forecasts against the same public records 1776 cites. The goal is attention triage and accountability, not gambling.
6. **Run on real, live data.** No mock data. The demo pulls from the Texas Register as it exists on the day of judging.

## 4. Non-goals

Ruthlessly out of scope for v1. Each prevents a specific failure mode we already identified.

1. **Not a meeting summarizer.** The market is saturated (citymeetings.nyc, Civic Sunlight, Citizen Portal AI, and many more). Summarization solves *length*; 1776 solves *accountability across time*. If it reads like a summary tool, we have failed.
2. **Not a rule-navigation chatbot.** That is SAM's job, and it is already deployed. 1776 answers a different question — not "what does the rule require of me?" but "how did this rule come to be, and was the public heard?"
3. **Not an accusation engine.** 1776 produces **leads with receipts**, never verdicts. It never asserts wrongdoing, corruption, or bad faith about a named person. Framing is "here is what the record shows"; interpretation is left to the human. (Defamation is the one bug that destroys the trust we're building.)
4. **Not multi-state or federal — yet.** One jurisdiction (Texas) done deeply beats ten done shallowly, and Texas is the strategically correct target: Vulcan's home state and the site of its SAM deployment.
5. **Not a comment *generator*.** We check comment integrity; we never manufacture public comment. (The opposite tool is part of the problem.)
6. **Not the full 157-billion-record legal graph.** We are not rebuilding Justinian. We ingest a bounded slice sufficient for the accountability task.
7. **Not a partisan scorecard.** 1776 is direction-agnostic: it works identically whether rules are being added or cut, under any administration. (See §11.)
8. **Not a real-money prediction market or gambling product.** 1776 may include civic forecasting, reputation, and play-money probabilities to help watchdogs prioritize attention. It must not accept deposits, pay prizes, create tradable financial interests, or imply regulated market/exchange functionality.

## 5. Users & jobs-to-be-done

Honest about who actually has the motive to act (the disengaged resident is a hope, not a user).

**Primary — the under-resourced watchdog.**
- *Statehouse / local reporter* with no institutional memory because the beat veteran was laid off. Job: "Tell me if anything in this week's Texas Register is a story — and hand me the receipts so I can write it today."
- *Civic advocate / nonprofit* tracking rules in one domain (environment via TCEQ, health via HHSC, energy via the RRC/PUC). Job: "Alert me when a rule in my area is adopted, tell me if the agency brushed off our comments, and tell me the comment window on what's still proposed."

**Secondary — the affected party.**
- *Small business owner / resident* directly regulated by a rule. Job: "In plain English, what is changing, does it affect me, and can I still comment?"

**Tertiary — the informed forecaster.**
- *Domain expert / advocate / former staffer / civically engaged reader* who understands a policy area and can add a probabilistic signal. Job: "Tell me what is likely to happen to this proposed rule, let me make a transparent forecast, and score me only against objectively resolved public records."

**The build serves the primary users first.** They have the platform to make an accountability finding matter, which is how a hackathon demo becomes real impact.

## 6. User stories

**Watchdog (reporter / advocate)**
- As a reporter, I want a ranked feed of recent Texas Register rule actions with a one-line "why this might matter," so I can triage a week of activity in minutes.
- As a reporter, I want to see, for an adopted rule, the public concerns raised, the agency's stated response, and whether the adopted text is consistent with it, so I can write an accountability story with sourced quotes.
- As an advocate, I want every claim in 1776's brief to link to the exact source document, so I can verify and cite it without re-doing the research.
- As an advocate, I want to be notified when a rule in my domain is adopted or when a proposed rule opens for comment, so I never miss a window.

**Affected party**
- As a small-business owner, I want a plain-language explanation of what a rule changes and whether it applies to me, so I can decide whether to act.
- As a resident, I want to know if a proposed rule's comment period is still open and how to comment, so I can participate before it closes.

**Civic forecaster**
- As a domain expert, I want to forecast whether a proposed rule will be adopted, changed, withdrawn, or affected by public comments, so watchdogs can see where informed attention is clustering.
- As a forecaster, I want every forecast question to have objective resolution criteria and a source of truth, so scoring is not arbitrary.
- As a reporter, I want to see crowd probabilities next to 1776's record-based findings, so I can triage emerging rules before the adopted notice appears.

**Edge / empty states**
- As any user, when 1776 cannot find primary-source support for a claim, I want it to say "not established in the record" rather than guess — so I never receive a fabricated fact.
- As any user, when an agency's preamble is thin or the source site errors, I want 1776 to show what it *does* have and flag what's missing, so partial data is still useful.

## 7. What the agent does (agentic workflow)

1776 is a genuine agent, not a static pipeline: given a rule action (or a natural-language query), it **plans an investigation, calls tools to gather primary sources, analyzes them, and produces a cited brief**, with a mandatory self-verification pass.

**Framework fit.** 1776 should use **LangGraph** for the explicit state machine and durable workflow: ingestion state, source retrieval, extraction retries, evidence gates, forecast resolution, and cite-or-cut verification. It should use **LangChain** for model/provider abstraction, tool definitions, structured outputs, prompt middleware, and tracing-friendly agent calls. Deterministic scraping, parsing, diffing, citation storage, and scoring remain ordinary Python services; the frameworks orchestrate those services rather than replacing them.

**The loop:**
1. **Plan** — decompose the task ("assess adopted rule X") into sub-questions: What changed? Who's affected? Adopted with or without changes from the proposal? What did the public say? Did the agency's response and the adopted text line up? What authorized it?
2. **Gather** — call data tools (§9) to pull the proposed and adopted Texas Register entries, the preamble's Comment/Response section, and the authorizing bill/statute.
3. **Analyze** — diff proposed vs. adopted text; extract each Comment/Response pair; classify the agency's disposition (made changes / declined / partial); cross-check the claimed disposition against the actual adopted text; detect scope/fiscal drift between stated intent and adopted rule.
4. **Forecast (for proposed rules / P1)** — generate resolvable civic forecast questions with explicit resolution criteria, e.g., "Will this rule be adopted with changes?" or "Will the final adopted text substantively address at least one public comment cluster?"
5. **Draft** — write the brief, attaching a source reference to every claim as it is written.
6. **Verify (cite-or-cut)** — a second pass strips any sentence not backed by a retrieved source. Uncited claims never reach the user.
7. **Emit** — the cited brief + the "were the people heard?" panel + provenance links + any forecast cards and resolution status.

**Agent tools (functions):**
- `search_register(filters)` — query recent Texas Register issues for rule actions (agency, TAC title, action type, date)
- `get_proposed(rule_ref)` / `get_adopted(rule_ref)` — retrieve the proposed and adopted preamble + text
- `get_comment_response(adopted_ref)` — extract the Comment/Response pairs and change disposition from the adopted preamble
- `resolve_authority(rule_ref)` — extract authorizing bill/statute citation → link to Texas Legislature Online for the bill + vote
- `diff(text_a, text_b)` — structural/semantic diff between proposed and adopted text
- `get_meeting_video(agency, date)` + `transcribe(url)` — statements layer (stretch)
- `cite(claim, source_ref)` — attach and enforce provenance (required for every claim)
- `generate_forecast_questions(rule_ref)` — produce only forecast questions that have objective, public-record resolution criteria
- `open_forecast(question, resolution_criteria)` — create a play-money/reputation forecast card for a proposed rule
- `resolve_forecast(forecast_ref)` — resolve a forecast from the Texas Register or another approved primary source
- `score_forecaster(user_ref, forecast_ref)` — update calibration/reputation using Brier-style scoring, never cash value

## 8. Requirements

### P0 — Must have (the demo is not viable without these)

**P0.1 Ingest live rule actions from the Texas Register.**
- [ ] Pull recent Texas Register issues and enumerate rule actions with metadata (agency, TAC title, action type: Proposed / Adopted / Emergency / Withdrawn, date, TexReg citation).
- [ ] For a chosen adopted rule, retrieve the adopted preamble and text, and the corresponding proposed entry it references.
- *Given* a valid Texas Register adopted-rule reference, *when* the agent ingests it, *then* it has the preamble, the Comment/Response section, and the proposed-vs-adopted texts available, or reports precisely which pieces are unavailable.

**P0.2 Plain-language explanation with impact.**
- [ ] For a rule, produce: what it changes, who it affects, and current status — in non-expert language.
- [ ] For a proposed rule with an open comment period, surface the deadline (30-day minimum under Gov't Code 2001.029) and how to comment.

**P0.3 "Were the people heard?" analysis (flagship).**
- [ ] Parse the adopted preamble's Comment/Response section into distinct (concern → agency response → disposition) triples.
- [ ] Classify each disposition: agency made changes / declined / partially addressed.
- [ ] Cross-check each claimed disposition against the actual adopted text (e.g., agency says "adopted without changes" — confirm; agency says "revised in response" — confirm the change is present).
- [ ] Note which sections were "adopted with changes" vs. "without changes to the proposed text."
- *Given* an adopted rule with a Comment/Response record, *when* the agent runs, *then* it outputs each public concern paired with the agency's response and an evidenced consistency check — framed as a finding from the record, never as an accusation.

**P0.4 Provenance guarantee (cite-or-cut).**
- [ ] Every factual claim in every output links to a specific primary-source artifact (TexReg issue + section, adopted preamble passage, TAC citation).
- [ ] The verification pass removes any claim lacking a source.
- *Given* any generated brief, *when* a reader clicks any claim, *then* they reach the exact supporting source; *and* no claim exists without one.

**P0.5 Usable interface.**
- [ ] A searchable/browsable view of rule actions and a per-rule brief view with inline citations and the "were the people heard?" panel. (Simple web UI is sufficient; substance over polish.)

**P0.6 Agentic orchestration with LangGraph + LangChain.**
- [ ] Implement the core investigation as a LangGraph state machine with explicit nodes for planning, source gathering, extraction, analysis, drafting, verification, and emission.
- [ ] Expose deterministic project functions as LangChain tools where LLM orchestration is useful: register search, proposed/adopted retrieval, Comment/Response extraction, diffing, authority resolution, and citation enforcement.
- [ ] Persist intermediate graph state and tool outputs so a failed or partial investigation can be inspected, retried, and rendered with clear missing-evidence states.
- *Given* a rule investigation, *when* the graph runs, *then* each major step is traceable as a node transition with input/output state and source references.

### P1 — Nice to have (strong fast-follows)

**P1.1 Authority lineage.** Resolve an adopted rule's authorizing bill/statute (e.g., "implements SB 2105, 88th Legislature") and link to the bill and roll-call vote via Texas Legislature Online / OpenStates.

**P1.2 Watchlist + alerts.** Let a user follow an agency/TAC title and get notified when a new proposed or adopted rule appears in the weekly Texas Register.

**P1.3 Ranked "why this matters" feed.** Triage view scoring recent actions by likely newsworthiness (scope of change, comments declined, fiscal magnitude, adopted-with-changes vs. without).

**P1.4 Scope/fiscal drift flag.** Compare the agency's stated fiscal/local-employment impact note against the adopted rule and flag material divergence.

**P1.5 Raw comment enrichment.** Where an agency publishes raw individual comments (e.g., TCEQ eComments, HHSC comment portals), pull them to independently verify the agency's Comment/Response summary rather than relying on the agency's self-report.

**P1.6 Civic forecasting layer.** Add play-money/reputation-based forecast cards for proposed rules, with objective resolution criteria tied to public records.
- [ ] Auto-generate 2-3 forecast questions per proposed rule, such as: "Will this rule be adopted?", "Will it be adopted with changes?", "Will public comments cause a substantive text change?", or "Will the agency withdraw or delay the rule?"
- [ ] Require every forecast to declare its resolution source before it opens (Texas Register adopted/withdrawn notice, TAC update, TLO action, or another approved public record).
- [ ] Show aggregate probabilities and confidence/liquidity indicators on the rule feed and rule detail page.
- [ ] Resolve forecasts automatically when the matching adopted/withdrawn/final action appears, with a human-review fallback for ambiguous cases.
- [ ] Score users with calibration/reputation metrics (e.g., Brier score, domain-specific accuracy), with no cash value, prizes, deposits, or transferable assets.
- *Given* a proposed rule with a valid resolution path, *when* 1776 opens forecast cards, *then* users can submit probabilistic forecasts and later see a source-backed resolution.

### P2 — Future considerations (design for, don't build now)

**P2.1 The statements layer.** Agency board/commission meeting and legislative committee video → WhisperX transcription (word-level timestamps, roster-anchored diarization) → link what officials *said* to how the rule turned out. This is the video-native differentiator and the "say" in say→vote→rule; architect the data model to accept it now.

**P2.2 Comment-integrity detection.** Detect synthetic/duplicative comment campaigns (the astroturf risk that AI comment processing makes acute).

**P2.3 Multi-state & federal.** Generalize the ingestion layer; federal via the Federal Register API and regulations.gov.

**P2.4 Model-legislation / provenance-of-text.** Detect where rule or bill language originated (industry template, another state).

**P2.5 Forecasting network effects.** Add richer civic forecasting features: domain leaderboards, expert panels, forecast rationales, dispute windows for ambiguous resolutions, alerts when crowd probability moves sharply, and cross-rule calibration by agency/TAC title. Keep this reputation-based unless legal review explicitly approves another model.

## 9. Data sources (all real, all live)

| Layer | Source | Access | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rule** (primary) | **Texas Register** — `sos.state.tx.us/texreg` | Scrape the date-based static HTML archive (`.../texreg/archive/{Month}{Day}{Year}/...`), split into Proposed Rules / Adopted Rules / Emergency / Withdrawn by TAC title; weekly RSS/email of new issues | The core dataset. Published weekly (Fridays) by the TX Secretary of State. **Flagship asset:** Adopted-rule preambles carry a semi-structured **Comment/Response** section, explicit statements of sections "adopted with/without changes to the proposed text as published in [date] (XX TexReg YYYY)," and the authorizing bill/statute. Static HTML — more reliable to scrape than a legacy DB-backed portal. |
| **Rule** (codified) | **Texas Administrative Code (TAC)** — `sos.state.tx.us/tac` | Free electronic access (mandated by HB 2304); searchable back to Jan 1, 1999 | The current, in-force text of a rule after adoption. 16 subject-matter titles. Use to show the before/after "in force" state. |
| **Rule** (historical corpus) | UNT **Portal to Texas History** — `texashistory.unt.edu` | Digitized back issues of the Texas Register | For building a historical corpus of proposed→adopted pairs during prep. |
| **Vote** (P1) | **Texas Legislature Online** — `capitol.texas.gov`; statutes at `statutes.capitol.texas.gov`; OpenStates | TLO bill/vote pages; OpenStates API | For linking a rule's authorizing statute to the bill + roll-call. Join key: bill/Code citations in the adopted preamble. |
| **Statement** (P2) | Agency open meetings (posted to SOS, appear in the Texas Register open-meetings section within ~5 min); agency commission video (TCEQ, RRC, PUC, HHSC); House/Senate committee video (`house.texas.gov`, `senate.texas.gov`) | Video URLs → local transcription | WhisperX on the DGX Spark: word-level timestamps for receipts, diarization anchored on the commission/committee roster + roll-call. |
| **Forecast resolution** (P1) | Texas Register, TAC, TLO/OpenStates, approved agency notices | Poll/scrape the same public-record sources used for 1776's briefs | Forecast questions may open only when their resolution source is known in advance. Preferred resolutions are machine-checkable: adopted with/without changes, withdrawn, comment period closed, effective date changed, authorizing bill action. |
| **Fallback** | `federalregister.gov` API; `regulations.gov` API | Free (Federal Register needs no key for basics; regulations.gov uses a free key) | Reliability insurance. Same proposed→comment→adopted structure, with a clean API, if Texas scraping blocks the P0 demo. |

**Access reality & the one honest caveat.** The Texas Register centralizes the *agency's summary* of comments and its responses inside the adopted-rule preamble — cleaner to parse than scattered comment forums, and it makes the flagship feature buildable in a weekend. But raw *individual* public comments in Texas are submitted to agencies through decentralized channels (agency portals, email) and are not all published in one place. So the P0 "were the people heard?" audits the agency's **self-reported** Comment/Response record against the actual adopted text — which is itself a sharp accountability question ("did the agency's stated reasoning hold up, and does the adopted text match what they claimed?"). Independently pulling raw comments to check the agency's summary is P1.5, done agency-by-agency where those comments are public.

**Jurisdiction decision:** Texas is the strategic primary (sponsor's home state and SAM deployment). The Federal Register API is the reliability insurance. Do not switch to federal unless Texas scraping blocks the P0 demo.

## 10. Technical architecture

```
┌─────────────┐   ┌──────────────┐   ┌────────────────────┐
│  Ingestion  │──▶│ Normalization │──▶│ Accountability graph│
│ (TexReg     │   │  (parse       │   │  (rules, proposed/  │
│  scraper +  │   │  preambles &  │   │  adopted, comment/  │
│  RSS + API) │   │  Comment/Resp)│   │  response, forecast,│
│             │   │               │   │  authority, sources)│
└─────────────┘   └──────────────┘   └──────────┬─────────┘
                                                 │
                                                 ▼
                       ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
                       │ LangGraph investigation workflow     │
                       │ plan → gather → analyze → optional   │
                       │ P1 forecast branch                   │
                       │ → draft → cite-or-cut → emit         │
                       └───────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                                       │
                                       ▼
                       ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
                       │ LangChain model + tool harness       │
                       │ structured extraction, tool calls,    │
                       │ prompts, model/provider abstraction   │
                       └───────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                                       │
                 ┌─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐
                 ▼                                           ▼
          Provenance store                           Web UI / API
          (every node keeps                          feed + per-rule brief
          source URL/ref)                            + forecast cards
```

**Accountability graph.** A lightweight store (SQLite is sufficient for the hackathon and mirrors the lineage-graph approach that worked in prior projects) holding: `rule`, `stage` (proposed/adopted), `preamble`, `comment`, `response`, `disposition`, `authority`, `agency`, and — critically — a `source` node attached to every factual node. Nothing enters the graph without a source reference. This *is* the anti-hallucination mechanism, and it matches Vulcan's own bar ("verifies and cites every claim").

**LangGraph workflow state.** The graph state should include `rule_ref`, `issue_url`, `adopted_text`, `proposed_text`, `sources`, `comment_responses`, `diffs`, `findings`, `forecast_questions`, `forecast_resolutions`, `draft_brief`, `verified_brief`, and `missing_evidence`. This makes failures inspectable: if a proposed entry cannot be linked, the graph branches to a partial brief instead of hallucinating; if a forecast lacks objective resolution criteria, it is not opened.

**LangChain tool/model layer.** Use LangChain to define the project tools, bind them to the selected model, request structured outputs for Comment/Response extraction and forecast-question generation, and keep model/provider choice portable. LangChain is not the source of truth; it is the harness around deterministic tools and typed schemas.

**Model choice.** Reasoning/synthesis: a frontier model via API for the agent loop and Comment/Response analysis. Cheap extraction/classification and all transcription: local models on the DGX Spark (128 GB) — WhisperX for audio; a local LLM for high-volume preamble parsing to control cost. The agent orchestrates tool calls; the verifier runs as a separate deterministic pass.

**Provenance enforcement.** The `cite` tool is not optional decoration — the emit step iterates every sentence, checks for an attached `source_ref`, and drops or rewrites any that lack one. This makes "no uncited claim reaches the user" a structural property, not a prompt request.

**Civic forecasting store.** Add `forecast_question`, `forecast_position`, `forecast_resolution`, and `forecaster_score` tables. Each forecast question stores its rule reference, close time, resolution criteria, allowed outcomes, source of truth, and status (`open`, `closed`, `resolved`, `ambiguous`). Forecast positions store probability snapshots rather than tradable assets. Resolution records must point back to the public source that settled the question.

## 11. Safety, legality, and non-partisanship

**Defamation firewall (non-negotiable).**
- Outputs are findings from the public record, never allegations about a person's motives or conduct.
- Language is bounded: "the agency's response states it declined to revise §X; the adopted text retains the proposed language" — not "the agency ignored the public" as a judgment, and never "official Y acted improperly."
- Every finding carries its receipt; anything unprovable is stated as "not established in the record."
- No open-ended "this looks suspicious" flagging. Only structured, evidenced comparisons.

**Non-partisanship.**
- 1776 is direction-agnostic. It reports on rules being **added and cut** with identical treatment, under any administration.
- **Sponsor sensitivity:** The Texas Regulatory Efficiency Office's mandate is deregulatory, and Vulcan's SAM supports it. 1776 must **not** be framed or demoed as "catching the deregulators," and it does not compete with SAM. Frame strictly as neutral public visibility into regulatory change — the accountability layer that navigation tools don't provide. A watchdog that only barks at one party is not a watchdog.

**Privacy.** Named individuals may appear in agency Comment/Response summaries; aggregate and cluster by concern rather than spotlighting private commenters. Officials acting in official capacity are fair to name factually (voted/said on the record); private citizens are not.

**Civic forecasting guardrails.**
- Forecasting is play-money/reputation only: no deposits, withdrawals, prizes, tradable assets, sweepstakes framing, or cash-equivalent rewards.
- Every forecast question needs objective resolution criteria before it opens. If the record is ambiguous, resolve as "ambiguous / no clear resolution" rather than forcing a winner.
- Forecasts are probabilities and triage signals, not allegations, endorsements, or legal advice. The UI must visually distinguish crowd forecasts from 1776's sourced record findings.
- Prevent manipulation basics: rate limits, one account per person expectation, audit logs, and moderator ability to void questions with bad criteria.

## 12. Success metrics & demo definition

**The demo that wins (this is the real success metric for the hackathon):**
> Point 1776 at one real, recently adopted Texas rule. Live, it produces: a plain-language "what changed / who's affected," the status, and the "were the people heard?" panel showing the public concerns, the agency's stated response to each, and an evidenced consistency check against the adopted text — every claim clickable to its source in the Texas Register. Then, for that same rule, show the authorizing-bill link (P1). The judges watch the tool reconstruct, from primary sources in seconds, the accountability analysis that a statehouse reporter used to take days to do — and that neither Justinian nor SAM does.

**Forecasting demo extension (if P1.6 lands):**
> Open a recent proposed rule and show 1776-generated forecast cards: "Will this be adopted with changes?" and "Will public comments cause a substantive text change?" Then show a resolved historical example where 1776 settled the forecast from the adopted Texas Register notice and updated forecaster reputation. This demonstrates that 1776 can help the public track rulemaking while it is still live, not only audit it after the fact.

**Acceptance bar:** at least one adopted rule where the tool surfaces a *genuine, sourced* instance of the agency declining a public request (or clearly adopting one), with the disposition verified against the adopted text. Confirm this case exists during pre-hackathon prep (see §14) so the demo cannot miss.

**If it became a product (leading indicators):**
- Watchdog activation: a reporter/advocate produces a sourced finding from 1776 within their first session.
- Coverage: # of adopted rules with a complete, cited "were the people heard?" analysis.
- Trust proxy: citation click-through (are users verifying? good sign) and zero substantiated hallucination reports.
- Forecasting signal: # of proposed rules with valid forecast cards, forecast participation by domain, calibration/Brier improvement over time, and % of resolved forecasts with unambiguous source-backed outcomes.

## 13. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Corpus cold-start** — the flagship signal needs an adopted rule with a real Comment/Response record and a clear disposition. | High | Pre-hackathon: hand-find 2–3 recent Texas adopted rules with a rich Comment/Response section where the agency clearly declined or adopted public requests. Pre-ingest them. Walk in knowing the demo exists. |
| **Preamble parsing variance** — Comment/Response formatting differs across agencies and issues. | Medium-High | Build the parser against several real agencies (TCEQ, HHSC, RRC, PUC) during prep; fall back to LLM extraction with the requirement that every extracted pair quotes verbatim from the preamble. |
| **Texas Register scraping** — no clean API; date-based HTML archive. | Medium | Static HTML is reliable; cache pre-fetched demo issues locally; treat live fetch as best-effort with cached fallback. Keep the Federal Register API path as full backup. |
| **Defamation** — a wrong or overstated finding about a named official/agency. | High | Cite-or-cut verifier; bounded language; leads-not-verdicts framing; findings restricted to record comparisons. |
| **Consistency-check false positives** — flagging the adopted text as inconsistent with the agency's response when it addressed the concern differently. | Medium | Require the model to quote the specific adopted passage it's judging against; "partially / unclear" as first-class outputs; human-verify the demo cases. |
| **Agent-framework overhead** — LangGraph/LangChain add moving parts during a short build. | Medium | Keep deterministic tools independent and callable without the agent; use LangGraph only for the investigation state machine; avoid deep multi-agent abstractions until the P0 flow works. |
| **Forecasting legal/regulatory ambiguity** — prediction-market language can imply gambling or financial-market behavior. | High | Use play-money/reputation only; no deposits, withdrawals, prizes, transferable assets, or consideration; call it civic forecasting, not trading; require legal review before any real-money model. |
| **Forecast manipulation / low signal** — early forecasts may be thin, brigaded, or overinterpreted. | Medium | Show participation/confidence indicators; weight by calibration over time; rate-limit accounts; separate crowd probabilities from record-based 1776 findings. |
| **Speaker attribution** (P2 statements layer) — bad room audio. | Medium | Defer to P2; when built, anchor diarization on roster + roll-call rather than voice ID from scratch. |
| **Scope creep** — trying to build the full say→vote→rule pipeline plus a full forecasting market in a weekend. | Medium | Enforce §4 Non-goals. Statements layer is P2, not P0. Forecasting MVP is cards + reputation only. The Comment/Response "were the public heard?" spine is the whole P0. |
| **Reads as a summarizer or a SAM clone** — judges pattern-match. | Medium | Lead the demo with the accountability finding, not the summary. First 30 seconds must show the "were the people heard?" panel. |

## 15. Open questions

- **[Data]** How consistent is the Comment/Response preamble format across major Texas agencies (TCEQ, HHSC, RRC, PUC, TDLR)? Which need custom parsing vs. general LLM extraction? (Affects P0.3 reliability.)
- **[Data]** Which Texas agencies publish raw individual comments (for P1.5 independent verification), and through what channels?
- **[Data]** Cleanest join between an adopted rule and its authorizing bill — parse the bill citation from the preamble ("implements SB 2105, 88th Legislature"), or match via TLO/OpenStates subject + session? (Affects P1.1.)
- **[Product]** For the consistency check, what threshold before showing a finding vs. marking it "unclear from the record"?
- **[Product]** Which forecast questions are valuable enough for v1.1, and which should be blocked because they cannot resolve from a public record?
- **[Product]** What reputation model should launch first: simple Brier score, domain-specific score, or a combined calibration + participation score?
- **[Technical]** Which model provider should LangChain target for the hackathon, and do we enable LangSmith tracing/evals during judging or keep traces local?
- **[Technical]** How much LangGraph persistence is required for the demo: in-memory checkpointing, SQLite-backed checkpointing, or full durable execution?
- **[Legal]** Exact bounded phrasings for findings — worth a short pre-written style guide so language never drifts toward accusation under demo pressure.
- **[Legal]** Exact boundaries for civic forecasting copy and rewards so the product cannot be confused with gambling, sweepstakes, securities, swaps, or commodities-event contracts.
- **[Sponsor]** Does Vulcan want 1776 framed as an explicit companion to Justinian/SAM in the pitch, or as an independent public-interest tool that happens to complement them? (Affects positioning, §2.)
